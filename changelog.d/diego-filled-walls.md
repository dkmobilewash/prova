### A wall drawn as one solid fill is read off the fill, on a sheet whose faces the finder did not read

Chrome reported Washington Village A.100 (held out) offering no candidates, with its walls
drawn as solid filled shapes. The stroke reader already emits a filled path's edges, and on
most sets they pair as faces. Surveyed over the 40 labelled training sheets: 24 carry
wall-shaped fill, and the pairer already covers at least 82% of every one's band footage
(Topeka p74 2,202 of 2,210 ft, Baker A100 605 of 617).

`filledWalls.ts` now reads the fill itself. Two edges of one dark filled area, 3 to 16 in
apart, with fill across the whole width between them and none just beyond, make a wall: its
centreline, with the fill's thickness. Rooms drawn as holes are read even-odd, and an outline
cut into short pieces is joined first. Not a band: a column or a stub under four times its
thickness, hatch strips, an area wider than 16 in, or a light tint. Bands go through the
finder's own filters beside the pairer's runs without changing which of those runs survive.
A stretch the pairer already found is cut out. Existing-wall rules are unchanged, so a band
in the legend's EXISTING WALL grey is set aside.

The shipping rule, per building: offering every leftover band on the training sheets
recovered 65 ft of missed wall and added about 200 ft of junk and unjudged lines (jamb ends,
the grey core of a wall already found). So a sheet is offered bands only when at least half
of its band footage is unread. No training sheet reaches that, and all 40 score identically
with bands on and off. Held-out totals, 18 labelled sheets: identical too, and none was
offered a band.

What this does NOT fix: Washington Village has no answer key, and a totals-only run on its
page 4 shows the finder reads no strokes there. It finds two drawings on the sheet and keeps
neither, so the zero comes from view selection, before any wall is looked for. That was not
diagnosed further, because the sheet is held out.
