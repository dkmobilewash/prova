### A wall's height by the roof over it: the roof plan's standing seams pick the section level

Joplin's sections print three levels (LOWER BOD 112'-0", ADMIN JBE 112'-4", CENTRAL TOW 116'-8") and its
legend sends A, B and L "to structure", so every one of those walls had no height: three answers and
nothing on the floor plan saying which. The roof plan says. A pitched metal roof is drawn as standing
seams, evenly spaced parallel lines; a flat roof is drawn with none. A pitched roof bears on the walls
at the sections' highest level, a flat one at the next level down.

Now, where the sections print two levels or more, the roof plan is lined up with each floor plan when
the legend's heights are read and its reading is stored per floor plan. In the review queue, a type
that runs to the structure takes, wall by wall, the roof over the saved rooms either side of it (the
taller roof where they differ) and splits into "under the pitched roof" and "under the flat roof",
each a SUGGESTION with its source: "roof plan A-201: standing seams, a pitched roof → A-301 CENTRAL TOW
116'-8" (floor 100'-0") = 16'-8"".
