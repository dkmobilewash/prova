### A line labelled CURB, PROPERTY LINE or SIDEWALK is site work, not a wall

The site drawn around a building pairs up like walls, and it was the largest kind of
new-build junk lying outside the building. Three geometric outlines were measured and all
three failed: a bounding box of the runs, the extent of the room tags, and the exterior wall
loop. Each one either took real walls or missed the site, because curbs and fences close
loops of their own.

The drawing's own label does not fail. A run is set aside, with the word as the reason, when
a site word (CURB, PROPERTY LINE, P.L., PARKING, STRIPING, FENCE, SIDEWALK, PLANTER, CONC.
WALK, ASPHALT, LANDSCAPE, RETAINING, SETBACK, EASEMENT) is written within 3 ft of it, and no
room tag or wall-type tag is within 8 ft. It uses the existing site-equipment step, so a
reviewer still sees the run and can take it back.

The equipment words' 100 ft length cap does not apply to these words. That cap exists
because a 10 ft reach found real walls beside a play yard. A word within 3 ft of a long line
is the line's own label, and a property line is as long as the lot.

Training, measured on what reaches the estimate: 319 ft of junk set aside and 0 ft of real
wall (Camp Bow Wow A101 199, Bunkie A100 71, Totem A2.1 50). Held-out, measured once: nothing
set aside on any of 26 sheets. The change is small and it is safe.
