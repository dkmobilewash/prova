### Takeoff: wall tags type the walls without the AI switch, and a tag wins over the thickness

Reading a wall tag printed in a symbol — a hexagon with S6A in it — is
free geometry; only the legend READ costs a model call. Until now the
symbol tags ran only with the plan-text read on, because the legend was
the only vocabulary they had, so a company with the switch off typed its
walls by thickness alone. Now the vocabulary is the company's OWN
wall-type codes always, and the legend's as well when one has been read.
On Baker's A101 that is 9 groups and 255 ft typed with the switch off,
where before it was 2 and 50; the switch on is unchanged.

And where a tagged group's drawn thickness is not the library's figure
(a legend's studs-and-board against an ICF wall with furring), the tag
wins: the group prices as the tag, the sentence names both numbers and
the other possibility (the tag may belong to the wall beside), and one
press — "Update C8B's thickness to 11-1/4"" — moves the library's figure
to what is drawn so the next untagged wall this thick can match. Only an
untagged group is ever matched by thickness, within a quarter inch as
before.

The check (switch OFF): a library holding the set's codes, Find the walls
on a floor plan: groups carry the drawing's tags and read "The drawing
tags these S6A…"; a group whose thickness disagrees reads "the tag wins,
so they price as C8B", its button says "Add as C8B — priced", and the
update button beside it changes C8B under Wall types.
