### A legend's heading no longer names the sheet, so Baker's ceiling plan is not measured

Baker A111 put 947 ft onto the job in the whole-set run. A111 is the reflected ceiling plan,
and the inventory's title reader named it "KEYNOTE LEGEND". The reader names a sheet by the
tallest lettering in the title-block region, and the keynote table's heading (26 pt) is taller
than the sheet's own title (16 pt). An unrecognised title is not refused, so A111 passed the
filter. A001, the demolition plan, was named the same way.

Refusing "legend" titles downstream was the wrong fix. HPL I-110..I-112 were also named
"KEYNOTES", and those are partition plans with a wall answer key, so they would have been
lost. Instead, a legend or keynote heading (up to two words before it, none of them PLAN)
never qualifies as a title, and the next size down names the sheet. A111 now reads
"REFLECTED CEILING PLAN" and A001 "DEMOLITION PLAN", and both are skipped. HPL's three now
read "PLANS TIER …" and stay measured.

Checked on all 36 training sets by inventorying every page, 1,831 sheets, with every sheet
treated as scaled. Only Baker A001 and A111 change verdict. Sixteen other sheets, all already
refused, lose the heading from their picker name: "MECHANICAL LEGEND" now reads "MECHANICAL",
and Irving CFP2's "LEGEND" now has no title.
