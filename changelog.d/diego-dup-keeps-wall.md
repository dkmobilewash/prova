### A wall and the counter in front of it are no longer "one wall offered twice"

Chrome reported a held-out hotel bath where a wall and a vanity's front edge run side by side
about 1.3 ft apart for the same 11 ft. That is inside the duplicate rule's 1.5 ft band, both
readings have both faces drawn so support ties, and length kept the counter, so the WALL
started rejected in the answer-key review.

Between 1.0 and 1.5 ft apart, within a quarter of each other's length, and only where support
did not decide, the rule now keeps the twin that behaves like a wall by at least two of three
signals: a pair an inch or more thicker, other runs meeting it at both ends, and a run on its
own line carrying on past an end. Under a foot apart, twins are still one wall read through
different faces and keep the old rule. A parallel run counts as a meeting only when it lies on
the line beyond an end, so a third offer of the same wall, or the counter itself, does not.

Measured by `zzDupWall.eval.ts` on each label file's own candidate rows, over the 408 pairs the
reviewer split on the 42 labelled training sheets. Training has no wall-and-vanity twin; its
nearest relatives are Topeka's kitchen peninsulas, counter on both sides. One pair changes,
on Clerk of Courts A-101.3: two readings of a glass storefront, and the rule now keeps the
18.3 ft run the reviewer took (353 of 408, from 352). No other building changes. Held-out
totals (20 sheets, the hotel set left out entirely): 109 of 147 before and after.
