### A roof edge over a lower roof is not a wall

A floor plan draws the roof below it as stipple, and the line where that roof
stops reads as a long, thin pair of lines, which the wall finder offers. The
tell is on the drawing: dots fill **one** side of it, the other side is clear,
and no partition tees into it along its length. A run that is at least 30 ft
long and at most 10 in thick, with six or more dots a foot on one side and
almost none on the other, is now set aside as "a roof edge", next to the
existing-work routes and with the reason shown.

Measured on the Round 16 keys, comparing this branch with the route switched
off:

| | set aside, junk | set aside, real | sheets moved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| training | **337 ft** | **0 ft** | Camp Bow Wow A101 181 · Bunkie 68 · cmb 57 · ELEVEN03 A4.B.1 31 |
| held-out (once) | 48 ft | 0 ft | O'Reillys only |

Every sheet that moved, moved by junk only. The 30 ft floor is set by the one
real wall the rule touched without it, 21 ft long. The rule does **not** reach
the USU and Riverbend sheets it was looked for on: 0 ft changes on all four.

Two other traits from those sheets were measured and not built. A run nothing
tees into does not separate junk from walls (1,394 ft junk against 874 ft real
at 40 ft and up). Walls drawn grey on a sheet drawn nearly all dark do not
either: K-4 A3.10's real walls are grey at 0.98 cover on a sheet 86.5% dark,
exactly like Camp Bow Wow's junk.
